# What is the Knowledge-Sharing Catchup skill?

## What is it about?

The Knowledge-Sharing Catchup skill runs a simple, private
knowledge-sharing series for one person, in any field.

Instead of learning alone from articles and courses, you have
regular one-to-one conversations with people doing the work —
and you ask them the same good questions each time, so you can
compare what they say, spot the patterns, and learn faster.

It works for a designer, a lawyer, a doctor, an engineer, a
teacher, or anyone else who wants to learn from people in their
field. You tell it your field and what you want to learn, and it
helps you write your own 10 questions. Each person gets their own
copy, starting empty, with their own questions, people, and
meetings. Nothing is shared with anyone else.

## How does it work?

1. **You choose what to learn.** You give your field, what you
   want to learn from people in it, how long each meeting should
   be, and what to call the series.
2. **You write your questions.** Together you write 10 open
   questions for your field — about what people are working on,
   how their process looks, what tools they use, and how they
   stay updated. A product-design example is included to show
   what a good set looks like.
3. **You meet people.** For each meeting, you get a call sheet:
   who they are, the time and call link, the questions, and a
   short professional Script — an introduction and a closing,
   personalised with their name.
4. **You take notes as you talk.** Drop rough notes in chat
   during the call and each one is filed under the right
   question, with just a short confirmation back so it does not
   interrupt you.
5. **After the call, it asks you first.** About an hour after the
   meeting ends, it asks whether it happened. If it did, it asks
   whether you have a transcript.
6. **Transcripts fill the questionnaire.** A transcript can be
   fetched from a recording service such as Fathom, pasted or
   uploaded in chat, or uploaded in the tracker from the
   person's details page. It fills the answers only where the
   transcript clearly says so, leaves the rest blank, and shows
   it to you to check before anything is saved.
7. **You get summaries.** Each meeting gets its own summary, and
   at the end of the series you get one all-meetings summary:
   the themes, what people agreed on, where they disagreed, the
   tools they use, and what it means for your learning.

## How will it be helpful?

- **It turns conversations into learning.** Without a system,
  good conversations fade. With the same questions each time,
  you can compare answers and see what really matters.
- **It fits a busy life.** One meeting at a time, at a time that
  suits you, with notes taken as you go and a summary written
  for you afterwards.
- **It respects your guests.** The Script gives you a warm,
  professional way to open and close every call, personalised
  with their name.
- **It never invents.** If a transcript does not say something,
  the question stays blank. What you read back is what was
  actually said.
- **It asks before it acts.** Nothing is added, marked
  completed, or saved from a transcript without you saying so
  first.
- **It works in any field.** A lawyer learns from lawyers, a
  doctor from doctors, an engineer from engineers. The system
  stays the same; only the questions and the people change.
- **It stays private.** Your people, meetings, notes, answers,
  transcripts, and summaries belong to you alone. Sharing the
  skill with someone else gives them the same system, not your
  series.

## Who is it for?

- Anyone returning to work or study after a break who wants to
  catch up by talking to people doing the work now.
- Anyone changing fields, or keeping up with fast-changing work,
  who needs to hear how practice has actually changed.
- Anyone who has good conversations and then forgets what was
  said — or cannot find their notes afterwards.
- Anyone building a network in their field and wanting each
  conversation to count for something.

## In one sentence

Meet people in your field, ask them the same 10 good questions,
file the notes, and end up with summaries that show you what
everyone is really doing — and what to learn next.
